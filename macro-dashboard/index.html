The Market Intelligence Hub (MIH) Global FMCG Macro Dashboard is a single-pane intelligence tool covering 47 markets across five regions: Americas, LATAM, WE, EEMEA, and APAC. It is designed for commercial strategy teams, category managers, and regional leads who need a rapid, data-driven read on macroeconomic conditions relevant to FMCG consumer demand.

Each market is assessed against 11 core macro indicators, then assigned an FMCG demand signal (Growing, Stable, Pressured, or Volatile) based on a rules-based methodology combining GDP growth, inflation, and contextual factors. The dashboard is updated quarterly aligned with IMF World Economic Outlook releases.

Target audience: Regional commercial leadership, market entry and portfolio teams, supply chain and trade planning functions requiring macro context for investment decisions.

Each market is assigned one of four FMCG demand environment signals based on a rules-based framework combining real GDP growth, inflation, and contextual qualitative factors. Signals reflect the expected near-term operating environment for FMCG businesses.

Growing
Real GDP growth >3% AND inflation <6%. Consumer demand expanding, volume growth likely. Favorable for investment and portfolio premiumization.
Stable
GDP growth 1–3% with contained inflation. Steady demand environment, limited macro risk. Suitable for distribution deepening and base maintenance.
Pressured
Low GDP growth (<1%) OR elevated inflation constraining consumer spending power. Demand under pressure; affordability key. Value-tier strategies relevant.
Volatile
Very high inflation (>15%), currency instability, or sharp GDP swings. High uncertainty; demand patterns unpredictable. Requires close monitoring and flexible pricing.

Note: Signal assignments incorporate qualitative override where quantitative thresholds are borderline, e.g., Argentina (Growing GDP, Very High Inflation → Volatile) or New Zealand (sub-1% GDP, low inflation → Pressured).

Forecasts, not actuals. GDP growth and inflation figures are IMF WEO October 2025 forecasts. Actual outturns will differ, particularly in markets with high policy uncertainty (Turkey, Argentina, Nigeria, Egypt).

Vintage lag. Current account data reflects 2024 actuals (latest available). E-commerce penetration estimates are 2024–2025 and may not capture rapid structural shifts in digital retail.

Big Mac coverage gaps. McDonald's does not operate in Kenya, Nigeria, or Bangladesh. Big Mac prices are shown as N/A for these markets. The Big Mac Index is a simplified PPP proxy and should not be treated as a comprehensive exchange rate assessment.

Aggregation risk. GDP and inflation averages mask significant sub-national variation. India's national GDP growth of 6.6% conceals major interstate disparities. Urban vs. rural dynamics are partially proxied by urbanization rate but not fully captured.

FMCG Signal is macro-only. The signal reflects macroeconomic conditions and does not capture category-specific dynamics, competitive intensity, regulatory environment, or channel structure. It is a starting point for market assessment, not a complete picture.

Internal use only. This dashboard is intended for internal strategic planning purposes. Data should be validated against primary sources before external publication or investment decisions.

FMCG Signal Methodology
Signal assessed based on GDP momentum, inflation trajectory, unemployment trend, and current account balance.

Growing — positive momentum across ≥3 indicators: GDP >3% AND inflation <6%.
Stable — mixed signals, no acute risks; GDP 1–3% with contained inflation.
Pressured — inflation >6% or unemployment >8% with weak growth; affordability key.
Volatile — high dispersion across indicators; inflation >15% or sharp GDP swings.
Data Vintage & Update Cadence
GDP growth and inflation figures are IMF WEO October 2025 forecasts updated with the January 2026 revision. Current account data reflects 2024 actuals (latest available). E-commerce penetration estimates are 2024–2025. Big Mac prices are January 2025. Population and urbanization data from the latest UN 2024/2025 revision rounds.
Limitations
National averages mask sub-national variation. Big Mac coverage excludes Kenya, Nigeria, and Bangladesh (McDonald's not present). FMCG Signal is macro-only and does not capture category-specific dynamics, competitive intensity, or channel structure.
